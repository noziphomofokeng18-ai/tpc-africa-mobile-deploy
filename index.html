So... what actually is this business?

You get a SIM card, then use it to connect the people around you with things they already pay for every day: airtime, data, and electricity. 1

What you're actually doing

You get one SIM card, and with it, your phone becomes a digital service point for your community. Customers come to you, or you go to them, and you help them pay for airtime, data, electricity, DStv subscriptions, and more. Every qualifying transaction earns you a 5% commission, paid directly to you.

As a builder, you do everything a retail seller does: your own SIM, your own customers, your own 5% commission. On top of that, you also introduce other people into the business. When someone joins through you, you earn a once-off bonus on their purchase, and you continue earning commission from their activity for as long as they keep trading.

What you'd earn

5%

You earn 5% commission on every qualifying transaction your customers make. Here's what that looks like in practice: if your customers buy R1,000 worth of airtime, data and electricity from you in a month, that's R50 in your pocket. Build up a steady base of 60 regular customers, and you could be earning R1,500 a month for yourself, without recruiting a single person.

As a builder, you earn from three separate income streams, each one stacking on top of the others:

How the "team" part works

You bring in Person A. Person A is now in your first generation. You earn 5% from their customer sales, forever, on top of what they earn themselves.

If Person A then brings in Person B, Person B is in your second generation. You earn 2% from Person B's customer sales too. Your own money isn't reduced by this. It's extra, on top of what A and B earn for themselves.

Where does this commission actually come from?

Think about the last time you bought airtime at Pep, topped up data at Pick n Pay, paid your electricity through Shoprite, or bought data straight from your banking app. Every single one of those transactions already earns a commission, for them. That's money that's always been built into the price of airtime, data, and electricity: someone is getting paid a percentage every time you buy.

Up until now, that percentage has gone to big retail chains and banks, people who already have plenty of other income streams. The Perfume Co. Africa saw it differently: instead of that commission disappearing into a large corporate account, why not put it directly into the hands of everyday people who want to build something of their own?

That's exactly what TPC Africa Mobile does. It hands you the same kind of commission that Pep, Pick n Pay, Shoprite, and the banks have been earning for years, except this time, it's yours.
